# Brain breaks for Radar (draft v2 to vet)

There are two kinds:
- **Subject breaks** are a quick 3–5 minute game or activity linked to the subject you're teaching. Most are played together on the projector: you drive, and the class calls out guesses.
- **Calm breaks** are a few breathing and grounding resets for when the room needs to settle.

**How to vet:**
- Put ✅ or ❌ after each name, and edit anything.
- Claude Code will only load the ✅ ones.
- Links marked **(check link)** were taken from news or teaching sites but couldn't be opened directly from here. Open each one once to confirm it works and isn't blocked by your school's filter.

**Before using a website in class:**
- Open it on your own device first and project it.
- Students don't need accounts.
- Check that your school's web filter allows it, and note any ads.
- Daily games only have one puzzle a day, so play them as a whole class.

## Geography

### Globle ⬜
- **Link:** https://globle-game.com/
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a mystery country on a globe with no borders. Each guess turns a country redder the closer it is.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Today's mystery country is somewhere on this globe. Call out a country. Hands up, and I'll take one guess at a time."
  2. Enter the guesses and point out the hot and cold colours.
  3. After each guess, ask, "Which direction should we go next, and why?"
  4. Stop at 5 minutes, or when you've found it.

### Worldle ⬜
- **Link:** https://worldle.teuteuf.fr/
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** guess the country from its outline. Each wrong guess shows the distance and direction.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Here's a country's shape. Talk to the person next to you for 20 seconds, then I'll take guesses."
  2. Enter guesses. Use the distance and arrow clues to reason out loud together.

### Travle ⬜
- **Link:** https://imois.in/games/travle/
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** get from one country to another by naming the countries in between, using as few as possible.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "We need to get from [country A] to [country B] by land. Which country should we go through first?"
  2. Take suggestions and ask "Why that one?" before entering them.

### Seterra map quiz ⬜
- **Link:** https://geoguessr.com/l/aus (Oceania quizzes; pick Australian states, capitals, etc.)
- **Years:** 7–9 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** click the place on the map as fast as possible.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Class versus the clock. Call out where to click."
  2. Play once, then challenge them to beat the time next lesson.

### Room map ⬜
- **Link:** none
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving
- **What it is:** the classroom becomes a map. Students walk to where a place would be.
- **Resources:** none (optional: N, S, E, W signs on the walls).
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "The front wall is north. The middle of the room is [Melbourne / Australia / the equator]."
  2. "Walk to where Sydney would be… now Perth… now Darwin." Change the places to fit the lesson.
  3. Ask 1–2 students to explain where they stood.
- **Seated version:** students point instead.

### Country chain ⬜
- **Link:** none
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 2–3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** each student names a country starting with the last letter of the previous one.
- **Resources:** none.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "I'll start: Australia. Next person needs a country starting with A. No repeats, 5 seconds each."
  2. Go around the room. If someone gets stuck, the class can help.

## History

### TimeGuessr ⬜
- **Link:** https://timeguessr.com/
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a historical photo. Guess the year and where it was taken.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Look closely at this photo. What clues tell us when this was? Clothes, cars, technology, buildings."
  2. Take 3–4 clues from students, then agree on a class guess for the year and place.
  3. Reveal, and ask, "Which clue was most useful?"

### WhenTaken ⬜
- **Link:** https://whentaken.com/game
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** similar to TimeGuessr. Guess where and when a photo was taken.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:** same as TimeGuessr.

### Wikitrivia ⬜
- **Link:** https://wikitrivia.tomjwatson.com/ **(check link)**
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 3–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** place historical events in order on a timeline, with three lives.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Before or after? Hands up for before, hands down for after."
  2. Place the card where most of the class votes, and keep going until three lives are lost.

### Chronophoto ⬜
- **Link:** https://www.chronophoto.app/ **(check link)**
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** guess the year a photo was taken by sliding a timeline.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Stand up if you think this photo is from before 1950, stay seated if after."
  2. Then narrow it down to a decade together.

### Human timeline ⬜
- **Link:** none
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3–4 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving
- **What it is:** students hold event cards and line up in the right order without talking.
- **Resources:** 8–12 cards or sticky notes with events from your current unit (write them before class).
- **Steps:**
  1. Hand out the cards. Say: "Without talking, line up across the front in the order these happened."
  2. Then the class checks the line and suggests swaps.
  3. Reveal the correct order.

### Who am I? ⬜
- **Link:** none
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** 20 yes/no questions to guess a historical figure from your unit.
- **Resources:** a name written down (or one student picks a figure from the unit).
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "I'm thinking of someone from our unit. You have 20 yes/no questions to work out who."
  2. Keep a tally on the board.

## Economics and Business

### Then and now prices ⬜
- **Link:** https://www.rba.gov.au/calculator (RBA inflation calculator)
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 3–4 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** guess what an old price is worth in today's money.
- **Resources:** projector and internet, plus 2–3 old prices (e.g. a 1970 loaf of bread, a 1985 movie ticket).
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "In [year], [item] cost [price]. What would that be in today's money? Write your guess."
  2. Enter it in the calculator and reveal. Closest guess wins.
  3. Ask, "Why is it so different?"

### RBA Inflation Explorer ⬜
- **Link:** https://rba.gov.au/education/resources/digital-interactives/inflation-explorer/
- **Years:** 9–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** the RBA's interactive on how prices have changed.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:** explore one item together and ask students to predict before revealing.

### Higher or Lower ⬜
- **Link:** https://www.higherlowergame.com/ **(check link)**
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** which gets more Google searches? A good way into talking about demand and consumer trends.
- **Resources:** projector and internet. **Check the topics before class**, as some can be off-topic.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Higher or lower? Stand up for higher, stay seated for lower."
  2. Go with the majority. Keep the streak going as a class.

### Logo from memory ⬜
- **Link:** none
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** draw a famous brand logo from memory in 30 seconds, then compare.
- **Resources:** scrap paper and a pen each.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "30 seconds: draw the [brand] logo from memory. Go!"
  2. Show the real logo on screen. Ask, "Why do brands spend so much on something you only half remember?"

### 60-second pitch ⬜
- **Link:** none
- **Years:** 9–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** pairs invent and pitch a product for a random everyday object.
- **Resources:** none (pick any object in the room).
- **Steps:**
  1. Hold up an object. Say: "You have 60 seconds with your partner to turn this into a product. Name, price, who buys it."
  2. Two pairs pitch, and the class votes with thumbs.

## Civics and Citizenship

### Federal, state or local? ⬜
- **Link:** https://peo.gov.au/resource-hub/interactives (Parliamentary Education Office: "Federal, state and local" game)
- **Years:** 7–9 · **Time:** 3–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** sort responsibilities to the right level of government.
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:** the class votes on each item (1 finger for federal, 2 for state, 3 for local) before you click.

### Will your referendum pass? ⬜
- **Link:** https://peo.gov.au/resource-hub/interactives
- **Years:** 9–10 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** adjust results to see whether a referendum succeeds (double majority).
- **Resources:** projector and internet.
- **Steps:** ask, "What's the smallest number of states we need?", and test the class's prediction.

### Agree / disagree line ⬜
- **Link:** none
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving
- **What it is:** students stand along a line from "strongly agree" to "strongly disagree".
- **Resources:** a statement linked to your unit (e.g. "The voting age should be 16").
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Left wall is strongly agree, right wall is strongly disagree. Stand where you are."
  2. Ask one student from each end for a reason, then let people move if they've changed their mind.
- **Seated version:** show 1–5 fingers.

## Calm (any subject)

### Long-exhale breathing ⬜
- **Time:** 1–2 min · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** none.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Feet flat, hands in your lap. Close your eyes, or look down at your desk."
  2. "Breathe in through your nose for 4… out slowly for 6." Count out loud for 6–8 rounds, slowing your voice.
  3. "One more on your own… open your eyes when you're ready."

### Box breathing ⬜
- **Time:** 1–2 min · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** none (optional: draw a square on the board).
- **Steps:** trace a square on the board. "In for 4… hold 4… out 4… hold 4." Do 4 rounds.

### Five-finger breathing ⬜
- **Time:** 1 min · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** none.
- **Steps:** "Trace up your thumb as you breathe in, down as you breathe out. Keep going across every finger and back."

### 5-4-3-2-1 grounding ⬜
- **Time:** 2 min · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** none.
- **Steps:** "In your head: 5 things you can see… 4 you can feel… 3 you can hear… 2 you can smell… 1 thing you're looking forward to." Students don't share answers.

**Rules for every calm break:** joining in is invited, never forced; eyes closed is optional; no touching.
