# Brain breaks for Radar (v3, rewritten after round 5 feedback)

There are three kinds:
- **Subject breaks** are a quick 3–5 minute game or activity linked to the subject you're teaching. Most website ones are played together on the projector: you drive, and the class calls out guesses.
- **Any subject** breaks work in any class. A class whose subject has no breaks of its own (e.g. Maths) gets one of these when you tap "Subject break".
- **Calm breaks** are breathing and grounding resets for when the room needs to settle.

**How to vet:** change ✅ to ❌ after any name you don't want, and edit anything. The app (src/config/brainBreaks.ts) must match this file; a test checks the names and steps.

**Before using a website in class:** open it on your own device first, check your school's web filter allows it, and note any ads. Students don't need accounts. All links were checked on 31/10/2026.

## Geography

### Globle ✅
- **Link:** https://globle-game.com/
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a mystery country on a globe with no borders. Each guess turns countries redder the closer they are.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Globle on the projector.
  2. Say: "There's a mystery country somewhere on this globe. Hands up to guess a country, and I'll type it in."
  3. After each guess, point at the colour. Say: "Redder means closer. Which way should we go next, and why?"
  4. Keep going until you find it, or stop at 5 minutes.
  5. Say: "Which clue helped us most?"

### Worldle ✅
- **Link:** https://worldle.teuteuf.fr/
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** guess the country from its outline. Each wrong guess shows how far away and which direction the answer is.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Worldle on the projector.
  2. Say: "Here's a country's shape. Talk to the person next to you for 20 seconds: which country is it?"
  3. Take a guess from one pair and type it in.
  4. Say: "It says 2,000 km to the north-east. So which country should we try next?"
  5. Keep going until you get it (6 guesses at most).

### Mystery street ✅
- **Link:** https://www.mapcrunch.com/
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a random Street View somewhere in the world. Students use clues to work out the country.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open MapCrunch and tick "Stealth" in its options, so the place name stays hidden.
  2. Say: "We've been dropped somewhere in the world. Look for clues: which side of the road cars drive on, the language on signs, the plants, the weather."
  3. Take three clues from the class and write them on the board.
  4. Say: "Hands up for each continent… now, which country?"
  5. Untick "Stealth" to reveal where it is.

### Map quiz race ✅
- **Link:** https://www.geoguessr.com/seterra (Pick Oceania, then a quiz such as Australia's states and territories or capital cities.)
- **Years:** 7–9 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** click each place on the map as fast as possible. The class calls out where to click.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Seterra and pick a quiz that fits your unit.
  2. Say: "It's the class against the clock. Call out where I should click. Wrong clicks cost us time."
  3. Play once, and write the time on the board.
  4. Say: "Next lesson, we try to beat it."

### Room map ✅
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving
- **What it is:** the classroom becomes a map, and students walk to where a place would be.
- **Resources:** nothing (optional: N, S, E, W signs on the walls).
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "The front wall is north. The middle of the room is Melbourne."
  2. Say: "Walk to where Sydney would be… now Perth… now Darwin… now Hobart." Change the places to fit your unit.
  3. Ask 1–2 students to explain why they stood there.
  4. Say: "Back to your seats in 10, 9, 8…"
- **Seated version:** students point instead of walking.

### Country chain ✅
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 2–3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** each student names a country starting with the last letter of the one before.
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "I'll start: Australia. The next person needs a country starting with A. No repeats, 5 seconds each."
  2. Go around the room in seat order.
  3. If someone gets stuck, the class can help them out.
  4. Stop when you get back to the start, or at 3 minutes.

## History

### TimeGuessr ✅
- **Link:** https://timeguessr.com/
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a real historical photo. Guess the year and where it was taken.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open TimeGuessr and start a game.
  2. Say: "Look closely at this photo. What clues tell us when this was? Look at clothes, cars, technology and buildings."
  3. Take 3–4 clues from students.
  4. Agree on a class guess for the year and the place, then enter it.
  5. Reveal the answer. Say: "Which clue was most useful?"

### Chronophoto ✅
- **Link:** https://www.chronophoto.app/
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** guess the year a photo was taken by sliding a timeline.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Chronophoto and start a game.
  2. Say: "Stand up if you think this photo is from before 1950. Stay seated if you think it's after."
  3. Say: "Now, which decade?" Take a few answers and move the slider to the class's choice.
  4. Reveal, then play the next photo.

### Before or after? ✅
- **Link:** https://wikitrivia.tomjwatson.com/
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 3–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** place historical events in order on a timeline. The class has three lives.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Wikitrivia and start a game.
  2. Read out the new event. Say: "Before or after? Hands up for before, hands down for after."
  3. Place the card where most of the class votes.
  4. Keep going until you lose three lives. Say: "Our score to beat next time is…"

### Human timeline ✅
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3–4 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving
- **What it is:** students hold event cards and line up in the right order without talking.
- **Resources:** 8–12 cards or sticky notes, each with an event from your unit (write them before class).
- **Steps:**
  1. Hand out the cards to 8–12 students.
  2. Say: "Without talking, line up across the front in the order these happened."
  3. Say: "Everyone else: is the line right? Who should swap?"
  4. Reveal the correct order and ask one student why their event sits where it does.

### Who am I? ✅
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** 20 yes/no questions to guess a historical figure from your unit.
- **Resources:** a person from your unit, written down where students can't see it.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "I'm thinking of someone from our unit. You have 20 yes or no questions to work out who."
  2. Take questions with hands up, and keep a tally on the board.
  3. If they're stuck at 15, give one clue.
  4. Reveal, and ask: "Which question helped most?"

### Fact or fiction? ✅
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** surprising statements from the past. Students stand for fact and sit for fiction.
- **Resources:** 5 surprising statements from your unit, some true and some made up.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "I'll read a statement about the past. Stand up if it's fact, stay sitting if it's fiction."
  2. Read the first statement, wait 5 seconds, then reveal.
  3. Ask one student who got it right: "What made you sure?"
  4. Do all 5.
- **Seated version:** thumbs up for fact, thumbs down for fiction.

## Economics and Business

### Then and now prices ✅
- **Link:** https://www.rba.gov.au/calculator/
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 3–4 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** guess what an old price would be in today's money.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start); 2–3 old prices, e.g. a loaf of bread in 1970 or a movie ticket in 1985.
- **Steps:**
  1. Open the RBA calculator.
  2. Say: "In 1970, a loaf of bread cost 20 cents. What would that be in today's money? Write down your guess."
  3. Enter it in the calculator and reveal. The closest guess wins.
  4. Say: "Why is it so different? What do we call prices going up over time?"

### Dollar Street ✅
- **Link:** https://www.gapminder.org/dollar-street
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** photos of real families' homes around the world, sorted by monthly income.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Dollar Street and pick a family, without showing their income.
  2. Say: "Here's this family's kitchen. Guess how much they earn a month. Low, middle or high?"
  3. Take votes with hands up, then reveal their income.
  4. Say: "What in the photo gave it away?"
  5. If there's time, compare the same room in a family on a very different income.

### Higher or Lower ✅
- **Link:** https://www.higherlowergame.com/
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** which gets more Google searches? A way into talking about demand and trends.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Higher or Lower.
  2. Say: "Does this get more searches or fewer? Stand up for higher, stay sitting for lower."
  3. Go with the majority, and keep the streak going as a class.
  4. Say: "Why do you think people search for that so much?"
- **Note:** Check the topics before class, as some can be off-topic.

### Guess the price ✅
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** guess the price of everyday items. Closest without going over wins.
- **Resources:** 3 everyday items with their current price (a supermarket website is fine); scrap paper or mini whiteboards.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Write down your guess for what this costs. Closest without going over wins."
  2. Hold up or show the first item, and give 10 seconds.
  3. Reveal the price. Hands up if you were within a dollar.
  4. Do all 3, then ask: "Why might the same thing cost more in one shop than another?"

### Logo from memory ✅
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** draw a famous brand logo from memory in 30 seconds, then compare with the real one.
- **Resources:** scrap paper and a pen each; the real logo ready to show on screen.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "You have 30 seconds to draw the Woolworths logo from memory. Go!" Use any brand everyone knows.
  2. Show the real logo on screen. Hold up a few drawings.
  3. Say: "Why do brands spend so much on something you only half remember?"

### 60-second pitch ✅
- **Years:** 9–12 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** pairs turn an everyday object into a product and pitch it.
- **Resources:** nothing (optional: any object in the room).
- **Steps:**
  1. Hold up an object.
  2. Say: "You have 60 seconds with your partner to turn this into a product. Give it a name, a price, and say who would buy it."
  3. Choose two pairs to pitch.
  4. Say: "Thumbs up for the one you'd invest in."

## Civics and Citizenship

### Federal, state or local? ✅
- **Years:** 7–9 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** the class sorts services to the level of government that runs them.
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "I'll name something the government does. Show 1 finger for federal, 2 for state, 3 for local council."
  2. Read one at a time: passports, schools, rubbish bins, hospitals, the army, libraries, police, the money in your pocket.
  3. After each one, reveal the answer. (Passports, army and money: federal. Schools, hospitals and police: state. Bins and libraries: local.)
  4. Finish with: "Roads." Say: "Trick question! All three look after different roads."

### Double majority vote ✅
- **Years:** 8–10 · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a mock referendum. To pass, it needs most votes overall and a majority in 4 of the 6 "states".
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Each table group is now a state." Make 6 groups.
  2. Say: "Here's our referendum: should homework be banned on Fridays? Hands up for yes."
  3. Count yes and no for each table, and write them on the board.
  4. Say: "To change the Constitution, we need most people across the country AND most people in at least 4 states. Did it pass?"
  5. Ask: "Why do you think Australia makes it this hard?"

### Agree / disagree line ✅
- **Years:** 7–12 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving
- **What it is:** students stand along a line from "strongly agree" to "strongly disagree".
- **Resources:** a statement linked to your unit (e.g. "The voting age should be 16").
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "The left wall is strongly agree. The right wall is strongly disagree. Stand where you are."
  2. Read your statement.
  3. Ask one student from each end for their reason.
  4. Say: "If you've changed your mind, move now." Ask one person who moved why.
- **Seated version:** show 1–5 fingers.

### Right or responsibility? ✅
- **Years:** 7–10 · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** quick sorting of things citizens can do or must do.
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Hands up if it's a right. Hands on your head if it's a responsibility."
  2. Read one at a time: free speech, jury duty, voting, paying tax, a fair trial, obeying the law.
  3. After each one, ask one student to explain.
  4. Finish with: "Voting." Say: "In Australia it's both. Why?"

### Mini parliament ✅
- **Years:** 8–12 · **Time:** 5 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** the class debates and votes on a quick classroom "bill".
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Here's a bill: from next week, the last 5 minutes of Friday lessons are free time."
  2. Ask for one speaker for and one against. They get 30 seconds each.
  3. Say: "All those in favour, say aye… all those against, say no." Count hands if it's close.
  4. Say: "The ayes have it" or "The noes have it." (Remind them it's just for practice.)

## Any subject

### Count to 20 ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 2–3 min · **Type:** perk up · **Noise:** quiet
- **What it is:** the class counts to 20, one person at a time, with no plan. If two speak at once, start again.
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Close your eyes or look at your desk. We're going to count to 20 as a class, one voice at a time."
  2. Say: "No order, no signals. If two people say a number at the same time, we start again from 1."
  3. Say: "One." Then wait.
  4. Stop when you reach 20, or at 3 minutes. Say: "Our best was…"

### Quick, Draw! ✅
- **Link:** https://quickdraw.withgoogle.com/
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a student draws on screen and a computer tries to guess the drawing in 20 seconds.
- **Resources:** projector and internet (open the link before you start).
- **Steps:**
  1. Open Quick, Draw! and press "Let's draw!".
  2. Choose a student to draw with the mouse or on the board screen.
  3. Say: "Can the class guess it faster than the computer? Call out!"
  4. Swap drawers after each round. Play 3–4 rounds.

### Stand up if it's true ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 2–3 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving
- **What it is:** quick true/false statements about today's lesson. Stand for true, sit for false.
- **Resources:** 5 true or false statements about today's lesson.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "I'll read a statement. Stand up if it's true, stay sitting if it's false."
  2. Read each statement and give 3 seconds.
  3. Reveal the answer, and fix any common mistake in one sentence.
  4. Do all 5, then say: "Sit down and shake out your hands."
- **Seated version:** thumbs up or down.

### Odd one out ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** four words from the topic on the board. Which one doesn't belong, and why?
- **Resources:** four words from your topic, written on the board.
- **Steps:**
  1. Write four words from your topic on the board.
  2. Say: "Which one is the odd one out? Talk to your partner for 30 seconds."
  3. Take two or three answers.
  4. Say: "There can be more than one right answer, as long as your reason is good."

### Alphabet race ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 3 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** pairs list a topic word for as many letters of the alphabet as they can in 90 seconds.
- **Resources:** scrap paper and a pen per pair.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "With your partner, write A to Z down the side of the page."
  2. Say: "You have 90 seconds to write a word from our topic for as many letters as you can. Go!"
  3. Call time. Say: "Hands up if you got 10 or more… 15 or more?"
  4. Ask the winning pair for their hardest letter.

### Board sketch ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 3–4 min · **Type:** perk up
- **What it is:** a student draws a key word on the board, and the class guesses it. No letters or numbers allowed.
- **Resources:** 5 key words from your topic, on slips of paper; a whiteboard marker.
- **Steps:**
  1. Choose a student and hand them a word.
  2. Say: "Draw this on the board. No letters, no numbers, no talking. You have 30 seconds."
  3. The class calls out guesses. Whoever gets it draws the next word.
  4. Play until the 5 words are used.

### Rock-paper-scissors cheer squad ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 2–3 min · **Type:** perk up · **Position:** moving · **Noise:** loud
- **What it is:** whoever loses becomes the winner's cheer squad, until there are two finalists with half the room cheering each.
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Stand up and find a partner. Play rock-paper-scissors."
  2. Say: "If you lose, follow your winner and cheer their name. Winners, find another winner."
  3. Keep going until there are two players left, each with a cheer squad.
  4. Play the final, then say: "Big cheer, and back to your seats in 10, 9, 8…"

## Calm

### Long-exhale breathing ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 1–2 min · **Type:** calm · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Feet flat, hands in your lap. Close your eyes, or look down at your desk."
  2. Say: "Breathe in through your nose for 4… and out slowly for 6." Count out loud for 6–8 rounds, slowing your voice down.
  3. Say: "One more on your own… open your eyes when you're ready."

### Box breathing ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 1–2 min · **Type:** calm · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** nothing (optional: a square drawn on the board).
- **Steps:**
  1. Draw a square on the board.
  2. Say: "Follow my finger. In for 4… hold for 4… out for 4… hold for 4." Trace one side for each.
  3. Do 4 rounds, then say: "Back to normal breathing."

### Five-finger breathing ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 1 min · **Type:** calm · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Hold one hand up in front of you. With a finger of the other hand, you'll trace around it."
  2. Say: "Trace up your thumb as you breathe in… down as you breathe out." Keep going across every finger and back.
  3. Say: "Rest your hands. Notice how you feel."

### 5-4-3-2-1 grounding ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 2 min · **Type:** calm · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Do this in your head. You don't need to share."
  2. Say: "Find 5 things you can see… 4 things you can feel… 3 things you can hear… 2 things you can smell… and 1 thing you're looking forward to." Pause after each one.
  3. Say: "Take one slow breath, and we'll carry on."

### Listening minute ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 1–2 min · **Type:** calm · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "For one minute, we'll be completely silent and count how many different sounds we can hear."
  2. Time one minute in silence.
  3. Say: "How many did you hear? What was the quietest sound?" Take 2–3 answers.

### Squeeze and release ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 2 min · **Type:** calm · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** silent
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Squeeze your hands into tight fists… hold for 5… and let go."
  2. Say: "Lift your shoulders up to your ears… hold for 5… and drop them."
  3. Say: "Press your feet into the floor… hold for 5… and relax."
  4. Say: "Notice how heavy and loose you feel now."

### Seated stretch ✅
- **Years:** Any · **Time:** 2 min · **Type:** calm · **Position:** seated · **Noise:** quiet
- **Resources:** nothing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Say: "Sit tall. Reach both arms up high… and slowly bring them down."
  2. Say: "Drop your right ear towards your right shoulder… now the left."
  3. Say: "Roll your shoulders back three times… and forward three times."
  4. Say: "One big breath in… and out. Let's get back to it."
